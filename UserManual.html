HotLap User Manual

A GPS lap timer and data recorder for track days, racing, karting, motorcycling and touring — on your Garmin watch.

Overview

HotLap turns your compatible Garmin watch into a motorsport lap timer. It records a standard Garmin activity (GPS, speed, heart rate, elevation, lap splits) and adds racing-specific data: live delta-to-best, G-force, per-track sectors and pit timing. You can time four ways — by crossing a circuit start/finish line, point-to-point from a separate start to a finish line (autocross, rally, hillclimb), by a set distance, or by tapping — and review everything afterward in Garmin Connect or export the FIT into third-party analysis tools.

HotLap is free to try (3 laps or runs per session, unlimited sessions). A one-time Unlock PRO purchase removes the cap. See Free trial & PRO.

Getting started

  1. Install & open HotLap on your watch and step outside for a clear view of the sky so it can get a GPS fix (the outer ring blinks yellow and the status reads NO GPS until it does).
  2. Open the menu — tap the screen (then tap again), press-and-hold, or hold the UP/MENU button.
  3. Choose how laps are timed under Lap Method (Circuit, Point-to-Point, Distance, or Manual). See Lap methods.
  4. In Circuit mode, tell it where the line is: either Set Track (pick a real circuit from the database) or Set Start/Finish Line (drop a line at your current spot). In Point-to-Point mode, use Select Track (a nearby point-to-point track) or Mark Track (build one on the watch) — see Point-to-Point. Distance and Manual modes don't need a line.
  5. Start. Setting a manual line starts recording immediately; picking a track drops you back on the menu with Start Session highlighted so you can begin when ready.
When no line is set in Circuit mode, the top of the Dashboard flashes SET LINE / SET TRACK and the outer ring blinks red — a reminder that lap timing isn't armed yet.
On a database track, lap timing waits for your first start/finish crossing — the lap out from the pits or paddock is your out-lap, during which the header blinks a yellow AWAITING S/F and the lap clock sits at 0. It isn't counted, so Lap 1 is a clean flying lap. In Manual mode the header blinks TAP TO START and your first tap starts Lap 1. A manual line (dropped where you're sitting, on the line) and Distance mode start timing immediately. Recording begins right away either way, so the out-lap is still in your saved activity — it's just not a counted lap.

Point-to-Point (autocross, rally & hillclimb)

Point-to-Point times a run from a start line to a separate finish line — the shape of autocross, rally stages, hillclimbs, gymkhana and sprints, where you don't lap a closed loop. Each pass is called a run (not a lap); the app shows "RUN" everywhere in this mode and you compare each run against your best. Set Menu → Lap Method → Point-to-Point first.

Getting a course

Two ways, both on the main menu in Point-to-Point mode:

  • Select Track — lists nearby point-to-point tracks from the database (only P2P tracks appear here). If none are nearby you'll see "No tracks found" — use Mark Track instead.
  • Mark Track — build a course on the watch. Ideal on an autocross course walk or a rally recon pass.

Marking a course

Open Mark Track and, standing (or driving slowly) at each point, choose:

  • Mark Start — stand on the start line and select it. It also captures your facing direction when you're moving.
  • Add Sector — drop an in-between split point. Repeat for each; Undo Sector removes the last one.
  • Mark Finish — stand on the finish line and select it.
  • Width — the catch width of the start/finish line (default 30 m); widen it for a broad gate. A 15 m minimum always applies so GPS wobble can't miss the line.
  • Save & Use — name the course on the keyboard, and it loads straight away (switches you into Point-to-Point mode).
Mark the start and finish while moving in the direction of travel (even a slow roll) so the crossing direction is captured — this is what tells the line which way counts as "crossing." Marked while standing still, a line still works via a direction-agnostic catch circle, but a captured heading gives cleaner, interpolated crossings. A standing-start begins on launch regardless, so the start heading mainly matters for flying starts and for finishes near the course.
Out-and-back courses (finish line is the same as the start line — you launch one way and return through it): mark Mark Start and Mark Finish at the same spot, but mark the finish while rolling in the return direction so its crossing heading is the opposite of the start's. HotLap recognises the shared line and uses the opposite directions (plus the minimum-run-time guard) to tell your launch from your finish.

Timing a run

Press Start Session (you can do this anywhere — even at the finish after a course walk). The header shows AWAITING START; the run clock stays at 0 until you go:

  • Standing start — stage (stopped) in the start zone, then launch. The clock starts the instant you move, timed from your launch — so sitting on the line doesn't cost you time.
  • Rolling start — if you never stop, it arms on a forward crossing of the start line instead.
  • Cross the finish and the run is recorded; it re-arms for your next run.

Between runs the Timing screen shows your last run (grey) with BEST beside it, so you can eyeball it while staging for the next one.

Manual override: if GPS ever misses the auto start/finish, a tap on the START/LAP button arms the run when you're waiting, or finishes it when you're running. The Dashboard shows a TAP TO START hint while awaiting. (The menu is reached with the MENU button or a press-and-hold in this mode.)

Managing saved courses

Menu → Custom Tracks lists everything you've marked. Select one for Use this track, Rename (keyboard) or Delete. Courses are stored on the watch and survive Reset (which only clears the active track), so your autocross course is ready for the next event.

Sectors in Point-to-Point use a tighter catch radius than circuits, because autocross/rally courses are small and often double back near themselves. Per-run sector splits, best run, delta-to-best and theoretical best all work just like a circuit.

Set Start/Finish Line

In Circuit mode, Menu → Set Start/Finish Line drops the line at your current GPS position and immediately starts recording. Use this when you're sitting on the start/finish and there's no database track (or you want a custom line). The catch zone is sized by the Lap Radius setting.

To re-mark the line, use Reset Session (this clears the line, laps and stats), then set it again.

Set Track (circuit database)

HotLap includes a database of 5,600+ real circuits worldwide — road courses, ovals, karting and their layout / direction variants. In Circuit mode choose Menu → Set Track: it lists the nearest tracks by GPS (you need a fix first). Pick one and HotLap sets the start/finish line for you at the real crossing, sizes the catch zone from the track's actual line width, and loads the track's real sector boundaries and pit lane where available.

After you select a track, HotLap returns to the menu with Start Session highlighted — the track sets the line but doesn't auto-start, so you can begin when you're ready. The selected track's name appears on the Stats screen and is saved into the activity file.

Long track names on the picker scroll left-to-right when highlighted so you can read the full name including the layout / direction variant.

View Track

Once a start/finish line is set — from a database track or one you dropped — Menu → View Track opens a satellite map of that track on your phone, with its points shown as coloured dots: red start/finish, blue sectors, and orange/purple/green pit entry / pit S/F / pit exit. For a point-to-point track the red dot is the start and a green F marks the separate finish. It's a quick way to confirm you picked the right layout and to see exactly where the points sit.

Your watch and phone need to be connected (it opens the page in your phone's browser); if the phone isn't connected the watch says so. This is your own device, so the map shows the track's real location.

Request a new track

Don't see your circuit, or spot one that needs fixing (Check Here)? Request it and it can be added in a future update. Email info@thesterk.com with:

  • A Google Maps pin to the track's start/finish. In Google Maps, right-click the middle of the start/finish line (on the racing surface), then copy the coordinates or the share link, and paste it into the email.
  • The track name (and the specific layout / direction if it has more than one — e.g. North, South, Short, Long, CW/CCW).
  • Any sector and layout information you have — sector boundary locations, pit entry / pit exit / pit start-finish points, and the approximate track width at start/finish all help. Extra pins or a sketch are welcome; even just the name and start/finish pin is enough to get it added.
The more precise the pins, the better lap and sector timing will be — but don't worry if you only have the start/finish; sectors will fall back to even splits until exact boundaries are added.

Sectors

Sectors split the lap so you can see where you're gaining or losing time.

  • On a database track with sector data, HotLap uses the circuit's real sector boundaries (up to 8 sectors). Your splits line up with the track's official sectors.
  • On a manual line or Distance mode, it falls back to 3 even splits by distance.

The Dashboard and Timing screens show live sector deltas versus your best; the Lap List shows every sector per lap. The theoretical best lap (sum of your fastest sectors) appears on the Timing and Stats screens.

Pit lane & pit stops

On database tracks that include pit data, HotLap detects when you enter and leave the pit lane and handles it intelligently so a pit visit doesn't corrupt your lap times:

  • While you're in the pit lane, the normal start/finish trigger is suppressed, so driving past start/finish on the pit road doesn't count a phantom lap.
  • The lap you pit on is tagged IN and the lap you rejoin on is tagged OUT in the Lap List; both are excluded from your best lap and best sectors.
  • A live blue PIT timer shows your stop duration on the Dashboard, Simple Lap and Timing screens.
  • If the track has a dedicated pit start/finish line (aligned with the real one), crossing it in the pit closes your in-lap right there and starts the out-lap — keeping lap continuity through the stop.
  • Fastest and total pit time are shown on the Stats and Results screens, and the pit count, best and total pit times are saved into the activity.

Screens

Page up/down to move between them. The one you land on at session start is the Session Screen setting.

Dashboard

An at-a-glance grid: current lap, sectors, speed, average, distance, G-force, lap count, temperature and battery.

Simple Lap

A big current-lap time, colored versus your best (green = ahead), a live predictive delta bar (a centered meter whose needle drifts green/right as you gain on your best and red/left as you lose, with the gap shown numerically), and your best lap.

Timing

Current lap, predicted lap, best lap, theoretical best, and live sector deltas.

Stats

Best lap, lap count, average/max speed, distance, max G / heart rate / temperature, ascent/descent, SpO2, respiration, pit best/total, and the selected track name.

Lap List

Scrollable lap history with sector splits and IN/OUT pit-lap tags.

Track Map

A live GPS trace that auto-fits and follows heading-up as you drive.

Trip

Available in Distance/Manual modes: moving time, distance, and distance/time back to your start — or remaining distance and estimated time to a Trip Target — plus ascent/descent.

Results

Shown when you finish: best lap + G for racing, or moving time + climb for touring, plus a pit summary.

Save, review & export

When you're done, open the menu and choose Save Session (or press BACK and pick Save & Exit). HotLap saves a standard Garmin activity that syncs to Garmin Connect like any other.

Export the FIT

In Garmin Connect (web), open the activity → the gear/settings menu → Export Original to download the .fit file. From there you can analyze it in third-party motorsport tools.

VBOX / Circuit Tools

A companion script, fit2vbo.py, converts a HotLap FIT into a RaceLogic VBOX .vbo for VBOX Circuit Tools — it maps GPS, speed, heading, height, heart rate and the G-force channels, embeds a start/finish gate so Circuit Tools auto-splits laps, and records the track name, pit summary and per-lap sector times in the file's comments.

Download fit2vbo.py  ·  requires Python 3 and the fitparse package. Run it with python3 fit2vbo.py activity.fit to produce activity.vbo.

What gets recorded

Alongside the standard GPS track, speed, heart rate, temperature, elevation and lap splits, HotLap writes these custom fields into the activity for analysis:

  • Per sample: lateral & longitudinal G, SpO2, respiration.
  • Per lap: max lateral G, max braking G, sector times (up to 8), pit-stop time, and an in/out-lap flag.
  • Per session: max & average speed, max lateral/braking/accel/combined G, max temperature, best lap, theoretical best, total ascent/descent, the selected track name, and pit count / best / total pit time.
The circuit name is stored in a dedicated track field in the activity — the activity's title stays your chosen activity type, but the track it was run on travels with the file.

Free trial & PRO

HotLap is free to try: run as many sessions as you like, each capped at 3 recorded laps — including full use of the track database. To record unlimited laps, unlock PRO with a one-time purchase (no subscription).

How to unlock

  1. Open the menu (tap the screen, or hold UP).
  2. Scroll to the bottom, just above the Version line, and choose Unlock PRO.
  3. Follow the on-screen instructions. Your watch needs internet to complete the purchase — either via Wi-Fi, or with the Garmin Connect app open and paired on your phone.

Troubleshooting / FAQ

HotLap is showing a swipe down lock/unlock icon when I open it.

This is a watch OS setting protecting activities from accidental touchscreen taps. To remove this - open the watch OS Settings -> Focus Modes -> Activities -> Touch Lock OFF.

It says NO GPS and won't start.

Get outside with a clear sky view and wait for the fix. In Circuit mode you can't start until there's a fix, because the line needs a position. Distance and Manual can start without one, but laps/track won't work until a fix arrives.

Laps aren't triggering in Circuit mode.

Make sure a line is set (the SET LINE / SET TRACK prompt should be gone) and that you're crossing in the correct direction — the direction check rejects wrong-way crossings. If you set a manual line, a larger Lap Radius catches wider crossings.

My pit lane pass counted as a lap (or my in-lap looks wrong).

Pit handling only works on database tracks that include pit data. On a manual line there's no pit lane, so a pass near start/finish will register.

The lap I pitted on isn't in my best lap.

That's intentional — in-laps and out-laps are excluded from best lap and best sectors so a stop doesn't skew your bests.

How do I re-mark the start/finish?

Use Reset Session (it clears the line, laps and stats), then Set Track or Set Start/Finish Line again.

The header blinks "AWAITING S/F" and the lap timer isn't counting — is something wrong?

No — that's the out-lap. On a database track the lap clock intentionally waits for your first start/finish crossing so Lap 1 is a clean flying lap instead of a partial pit-out lap; in Manual mode it blinks TAP TO START and waits for your first tap. You're still recording the whole time (the out-lap is in your saved activity), and timing starts automatically the moment you cross the line or tap. Because a real pit exit is usually past start/finish, this can persist for most of the out-lap — that's expected. A manual line and Distance mode start immediately, so they never show it.

It started recording on its own — why?

That's Auto Rec Session (on by default). Once you've set up a session, HotLap starts recording automatically when you're moving (about 15 mph, held for a few seconds) so a forgotten Start can't cost you a run — and it lands you on the session screen so you know. If it fired when you didn't mean to record (driving to tech, repositioning the car), use Reset Session to discard and re-arm it, or turn the setting off in the menu. It needs a GPS fix and, in Circuit mode a line or track (in Point-to-Point, a start+finish course).

Why do my sectors show "Reverse - Sectors OFF" on the watch?

HotLap checks the direction you cross the start/finish line against the selected track's layout. If you're running the track the opposite way, the sector splits would be out of order, so HotLap turns them off and blinks a yellow Reverse - Sectors OFF notice wherever splits normally appear — but your lap times still record normally. This is decided at your first start/finish crossing and stays set for the rest of the session. If it's unexpected, you likely selected the wrong direction of the track (for example the CW vs CCW version): use Reset Session and reselect the correct layout. If you're intentionally running the reverse direction, this is expected and you can ignore it.

What is GNSS Mode, and which should I choose?

GNSS Mode controls which satellite systems the watch uses. Auto uses your watch's default. Max accuracy enables multi-band, multi-constellation positioning where your watch supports it, for the tightest fixes and the most precise lap and sector timing — at a higher battery cost. Battery saver uses single-band GPS only. For timed track sessions, Max accuracy is recommended; for all-day use on battery, Auto or Battery saver. The setting takes effect immediately and falls back automatically on watches that don't support a given option.

What sampling rate does the GPS use?

Current Garmin watch hardware delivers GPS data to apps at 1 Hz — one position fix per second. That's plenty for an on-wrist device, but detailed performance analysis, and the professional race-timing setups that need it, typically rely on a standalone 10 Hz or 25 Hz GPS unit. To get the most out of each fix, HotLap doesn't just grab the nearest sample — it interpolates the exact moment you cross the start/finish and sector lines between the two surrounding fixes, so your lap and sector times are far more precise than a raw 1 Hz reading would suggest.

My first lap shows no sector deltas.

Live sector deltas compare against your best, so HotLap needs one completed lap as a reference — the sector strip reads "--" until then and fills in from your second lap onward. (Your first lap's sector times are still recorded and appear in the Lap List.)

Can HotLap speak my lap number or sector times to my connected headphones?

Not currently — and it's a platform limitation, not something HotLap can switch on. Connect IQ (the watch app system HotLap is built on) does not give apps any text-to-speech or custom-audio capability, and it doesn't let an app send its own sound to Bluetooth headphones paired to the watch. Apps can only play the watch's short built-in tones and vibration. HotLap uses those for lap and alert feedback (toggle them with the Sounds and Lap Beep settings), and shows your lap/run number, lap time and live sector deltas on screen. Spoken prompts such as "Lap 4, plus three tenths" would require a text-to-speech feature that Garmin has not opened to third-party apps; if that changes in a future Connect IQ release, we'll look at adding it.

Which watches are supported / where are new features?

HotLap runs on a wide range of Connect IQ devices. For device support, pricing and the latest updates, check the app's Connect IQ store listing.

Safety

HotLap is a timing and data tool for use on closed courses, private property, and other appropriate venues. Don't interact with your watch while operating a vehicle or other motorized device — keep your eyes up and hands on the controls. Always obey traffic and venue rules and operate within your abilities. Use at your own risk.
